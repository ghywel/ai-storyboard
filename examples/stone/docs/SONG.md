# The Stone at the Bottom of the Sea

A song and music video about Yap's rai stones, money as shared agreement, and the work that money's scoreboard does not
see. Brief: Knight Commander Gareth, 2026-10-02: a photo of a rai stone and the prompt text he pasted. Capture the complexity and the
differing opinions, and keep a positive message that still challenges.

**His style call:** the song style, semi-rapping, tempo and beat, female vocal, lyric style and anime character
visuals of "Evil Plan" (Bright Mirror with Claude, https://youtu.be/oBE-lhubQ2k).

What Evil Plan is, re-measured today:
- about 140 bpm, B♭ minor;
- 966 words in 6:18, about 4 syllables a second while singing;
- rapped verses and sung hooks, first person, playful and self-aware;
- an anime heroine in illustrated close-ups and full-body shots;
- big kinetic lyric type landing on the beat;
- particles: words and objects breaking into grain and re-forming;
- staged scenes and a quiet section that lets the loud ones land.

We take the style only. The lyrics, character and story here are new.

**The narrator** is the sunken rai stone herself, as an anime girl. Nobody has seen her for generations and she is still
the richest rock on the island, because everyone agrees she is there. Up the road, a mother everybody sees every day
is a zero in the ledger. Her own story becomes the argument.

**The visual rhyme:** a zero in the ledger is a ring around nothing, and so is a rai stone, a disc with a hole. The film
passes through that hole.

## The song

About 140 bpm. Minor-key verses, a major-key anthem in the chorus, a lift for the last one. Female lead, semi-rapped
verses, sung hooks, one quiet spoken interlude. About 6–6½ minutes.

## What the song says, and why (the record behind it)

Every claim from the brief, with how sure it is:
- [R] marks the brief's own text;
- [U] marks well-known accounts not re-opened today.

- [R] Rai are limestone discs, mostly quarried on Palau about 400 km away, carried by canoe and raft. The hole takes a
  pole for carrying. Large ones rarely move: ownership changes by shared agreement and memory.
- [R]/[U] The sunken stone still counted as money. The story is in William Furness's *The Island of Stone Money*
  (1910), and Milton Friedman retold it (1991).
- [R] In the late 1800s David O'Keefe shipped stones in bulk; they were valued less because they were easier to get.
- [R] The stones are still used in ceremonial exchanges such as marriages and settlements.
- [U] Pigou's paradox: marry your housekeeper and national income falls, though the work is the same.
- [U] Marilyn Waring, *If Women Counted* (1988); Simon Kuznets warned that national income is not a measure of welfare.
- [U] "Money is memory": Narayana Kocherlakota, *Money Is Memory* (Journal of Economic Theory, 1998). Money as a
  record of who did what for whom.
- [U] Lifeboat crews (the RNLI in Britain and Ireland) are mostly volunteers.
- Verse 3's ledger on a million machines is a blockchain. The comparison of Yap's shared record to a distributed ledger
  is a common one, not a claim of this song.
- The rider and the home-cooked bowl are Pigou's paradox in modern dress: the paid delivery is counted in national
  income; the same meal cooked at home is not.
- The three positions in the bridge are the brief's own:
  - count care (satellite accounts, credits);
  - don't price it (commodification);
  - don't let one number be the scoreboard.

  The song takes no side between them. Its claim is that all three are carrying the same stone.

No figures for the value of unpaid work are sung: published estimates vary widely with the method used.
