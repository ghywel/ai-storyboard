# The Stone at the Bottom of the Sea (v2): "THE DIVER"

The take: "version 2 - 3min21s", the short script. The film is the Suno take alone, 3:20.8, plus 7 s of silence for
the credits. The take ends on a hard stab right after the last word, so the credits need room. His word: "go straight
into the music video", so there is no prologue, no title card and no bells. The style and direction are v3's
(`TREATMENT-v3.md`, `SCENE-GUIDE.md` "THE DIRECTION"). How this film differs from v1 is in `VARIANTS.md`: v1 is a TV
show, and v2 is a story.

## The idea in one paragraph

At dusk a girl in a mask and snorkel dives off the jetty with a torch.
- Her beam sweeps past a stone on the seabed: "Hi. You can't see me." It swings back and catches Rai, who freezes,
  sweats, and grins.
- They meet in a call and response of bubbles on the take's "na-na-na" vocalise.
- Rai tells her story by lighting her heart and projecting it onto the dark water like a magic lantern: the quarry,
  the crossing, the pole, the storm, the island that never wrote her off.
- They ride a manta over the reef past everything that is waiting, to the trader's rusting iron wreck and its hold of
  identical stones. The girl's own breath-held swim is the crossing.
- She surfaces into the harbour town at night: the bank machine, phones, a humming container, the night market. Rai
  rides along as a little holed pebble on a cord round her neck, and her chibi face pops up in it when she talks.
- At four in the morning the girl is the child with the fever, and her mother sits awake beside her.
- On the shelf, a ledger book opens its eye, looks at the mother, blinks, and writes 0.
- The fever turns the girl's doll's house into Pigou's paradox, then floods the bedroom gently into the sea. Rai sits
  at the end of her bed, and the 0 floats off the page into her heart.
- At dinner the family argue in three directions round a round table. When they all stand up at once, the table rises
  with them: every one of them is carrying it.
- At night the girl leads the village to the shore with lanterns. Divers thread the pole and ropes, and they lift.
- At dawn Rai breaks the surface and is carried up the beach to the girl's house. The girl hands her mother a slate
  with the nights counted on it. The sun rises through the hole in Rai's heart.
- Years later the girl is the bride, the stone stands in flowers, and her mother is old in the front row.

## Why this take

v2 has the bigger dramatic arc:
- a darker, quieter intro;
- the fastest rap of all three takes;
- a hushed pre-chorus dip in D minor;
- the chorus tag sung three times;
- a bridge sung out in F major;
- one dark breakdown (D minor);
- the loudest peak of either short take (the final chorus, −12.5 LUFS);
- an intimate outro.

A story with a child in it suits that arc: dark and fast at first, tender in the hush, huge at dawn, quiet at the
wedding.

## Tone

- Tender and funny, like a good children's film for adults. The jokes come from Rai, and the heart comes from the
  girl and her mother.
- The challenge is the same as v3's and v1's, told through one family: the ledger writes 0 for the woman we have
  watched love her daughter all night.
- Rai is never hurt. The pole is threaded with a wink, and she enjoys being carried.
- Rai is the only face. The girl is a faceless silhouette, and her mask is a dark lens with a moving glint that does
  the work of eyes (it flashes on "wow" and droops when sad). Everyone else is a faceless `person()` that emotes.

## Typography and the lyric

- **Sung lines:** bottom-band karaoke (Archivo bold, as v3). Each word rises into its place like a bubble as it is sung
  (a small upward drift and wobble before it settles), then fills.
  - Sung colour: yellow by default, pink in the tender sections, gold at dawn.
  - Unsung: 62%.
  - Readable everywhere, on a soft dark band when the picture is busy.
- **Spoken lines** (the intro, the outro): Cormorant italic, said word by word, with no box.
- **Hits** are slammed or written in the world, not on graphics:
  - chalk on the girl's slate;
  - ink in the ledger book;
  - words in the heart-lantern's light;
  - paint on the wreck's rusted hull.
- **Machines** (the bank machine, the meter): IBM Plex Mono.

## Camera and compositor grammar (the nuance)

- **Cinematic, not televisual:**
  - slow push-ins and pull-backs;
  - focus pulls (foreground reeds or bubbles soft as the subject sharpens);
  - match cuts on the ring (torch beam → the heart-lantern's circle; the 0 → Rai's heart → the sunrise in her hole);
  - underwater caustics and god rays;
  - a dream ripple transition into and out of the fever.
- **In the rapped verses:** hard cuts about every 2 beats inside the story (inside the lantern's circle, around the
  wreck). The frame keeps moving: a manta's bank, a torch sweep.
- **The `_post.ts` kit as before:** smooth capped shakes on real hits only (the storm, the grandad's fist on the dinner
  table, the breach at dawn), punches on slams, colour kicks on two or three impacts, motion blur from the
  render.
- **Bubbles in the foreground** for depth, and a gentle grain.

## Motifs

1. **Rai the friend and storyteller.** Her full acting range, with a partner:
   - wow when the girl appears;
   - smug and cheeky as she tells her legend;
   - soft at the touch;
   - joyous on the manta;
   - fierce at the ledger book;
   - protective at the bedside;
   - silly at dinner;
   - love at dawn.
   
   On land she is the **pendant pebble**, a small holed stone on a cord round the girl's neck, with chibi Rai popping
   out of it in a little bubble when she speaks.
2. **The ring:**
   - the torch's circle of light;
   - the heart-lantern's circle;
   - the 0 in the ledger book;
   - Rai's heart-hole;
   - the round dinner table with the lamp cord through its hole;
   - the sun rising through her heart.
3. **The slate.** The girl's dive slate on a cord, with a pencil:
   - she draws Rai on it in the pre-chorus;
   - she maps her own crossing on it at the wreck;
   - at dawn it carries the night's numbers to her mother.
4. **The ledger book.** An old bound account book with an eye on its cover. It lives on the shelf among the girl's
   storybooks (a clue in every bedroom shot before it wakes). It looks, blinks and writes. It returns in the dream,
   where its 0 floats away.
5. **The mother.** A faceless silhouette with a long plait and a shawl (not v1's woman: a different person, a
   different look). She dries the girl's hair at dusk, sits awake at four, sleeps in the chair in the dream, sits
   silent at dinner, holds a lantern at night, cries at dawn, and is old at the wedding.
6. **The pole.** It goes through Rai's heart in the lantern's story (the girl winces for her, and Rai winks) and in the
   night dive, threaded by the village's divers.

- **15 "nobody's seen me but everybody believes;":** they burst through a school of fish that parts around them.
