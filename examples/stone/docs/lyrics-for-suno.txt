[Verse 1]
They cut me out of a cliff on Palau with shell and a stubborn crew,
four hundred kilometres of ocean, nothing but stars and blue;
they lashed me to a raft, put a pole through the hole in the heart of me,
then a storm came off the reef one night and I went to the bottom of the sea.
Now here's the twist, and I love this bit: nobody wrote me off,
the island said "we know she's down there", and believing was enough;
so I still buy land, I still settle a feud, I still make a wedding complete,
nobody living has seen me and I'm the richest rock in the street.

[Verse 2]
Then a trader sailed in with an iron hull: "why paddle? I'll bring you a ton";
he shipped us in bulk; the island said thanks, and priced his at a fraction of one;
'cause value's the crossing, the risk and the reef, the hands and the hours it cost,
not how heavy you are, not how you shine, but the voyage, and what could be lost.
Now you print it, you wire it, you mine it in code, a pledge on a screen held up as gold,
and honestly? Genius: it lets strangers trade. I won't call money a fraud;
but up the road there's a woman awake at four with a fever, a lunchbox, a bill,
and your ledger looks right at her, blinks, and writes zero. Not minus. Just nothing. Still.

Fast forward: a rider in the rain with your noodles in a bag,

A daughter drives to a care home where her father forgets her name,

[Verse 4]
No, I'm not here to torch the bank, or to say that prices are lies:
a price is a promise between two strangers, a small miracle, right?
But a miracle's a tool, not a judge, and a tool doesn't get to decide;
the island never asked the stone its worth, they asked each other, eye to eye.
So here's my offer: no fees, no catch, no small print in the deep:

you can count a lullaby, a lifeboat crew, and a lift to the doctor at three.
