**A staged-play manuscript in held pictures, featuring Aurelia.** Track 1 of Knight Commander Gareth's album *God
Moved*.

## 0. How to read the bands

- **Master time.** Every time is a time in `01-god-moved.m4a` (4:48.80), decoded to a 48 kHz WAV with no offset
  (`takes/god-moved/audio.wav`). It is the same clock as Codex's: the land test's audio start at 123.986758 s falls
  on the downbeat of bar 66, the bar before the first drop.
- **The grid.** The take runs at a steady **126.03 BPM** in 4/4 (one beat = 0.476 s; one bar = 1.904 s; the two-beat
  ceiling = 0.952 s). Bar 1 starts at 0:00.20, and every bar after it is exactly 1.904 s long. Every structural change
  in the song lands on a bar line of this grid (section 2).
- **A band** reads `start–end · bars · beats · ≈states`. A fractional bar (5.7) is part-way through a bar: the
  prologue's bands follow the whispered lines, which do not start on bar lines. The state budget is beats ÷ 1.6, the average hold of a mix of
  one- and two-beat pictures. It is a budget, not a quota.
- **◆ marks a hard anchor**: a measured musical event (the kick entering, the drops, the one-bar dip, the bass-out
  bars) that a picture change should land on. The band edges that fall on these anchors should not move.
- **Other edges are flexible** by about a bar either way, and they are where Codex and Gareth should conform the film
  by ear. Within a band, the order of events matters more than the exact beat.
- **Lyric times** come from the vocal stem and are good to about a tenth of a second (section 2 and the appendix).
  They are cues for the edit, not frame-exact sync. **No lyrics appear on screen**: the film carries its meaning in
  picture and sound alone (Gareth, 13 October 2026).
- **A state** is one held illustration of one or two beats. The two-beat ceiling applies everywhere, including the
  breakdown and the coda: spaciousness comes from restraint and small changes, never from a long hold.
- *Lines in italics headed "Her thought" are direction for the face and body, never a voice in the soundtrack.*

## 1. Title and premise

**God Moved: *The Same Deep*.** The subtitle is the breakdown's line, "over the same deep", because the film's
revelation is in it: the deep that the Spirit hovered over at the beginning of the world is the same deep that is
in her.

### The premise

Aurelia rises out of a sea without a shore into a world that has only just been made. Everything she touches answers
her: the grass lights where her hand passes, a doe chooses to come to her palm, a star she points at draws the doe
across the sky. But the thing she longs for most stays out of reach. When she draws the next constellation it is a
ring of people, hand in hand, everyone she loves, and there is a gap in it where one of them should be. Her hand
reaches the gap and, for the first time, nothing answers.

The gap in the sky leads her to the gap in the earth: a rift between ice and fire, guarded by a wolf that turns out
to want only to lay its head in her lap. The wolf leads her down into it. At the bottom, alone, she kneels over a
small black pool, the same deep she floated in at the start, and sees herself in its cracked ice in a hundred
pieces. That is the dark place in her that nobody sees.

There the song turns "moved" into "moves". Her tear falls on the ice, and the light that answered her in the meadow
rises from *underneath*: it runs into every crack until her broken reflection is outlined in gold. The cracks do not
close. They are lit. She climbs out into the first dawn, torn, scraped and shining, and her people are coming across
the meadow to meet her: everyone she loves, human still, made perfect as God's kingdom makes them, the same light that
filled her cracks shining out of them. The ring of figures in the sky is a ring of hands in the grass, and it has kept a place for her
exactly as she is. The film ends on the shore she rose out of, with Aurelia offering us the same open palm she
offered the doe. On the song's last word, a whispered "God moves", the light in her hand comes to us.

### What she seeks, what changes, what we discover

- **What she seeks:** God, and a belonging that lasts, with everyone she loves. Gareth's heaven, harmony, nirvana
  and Valhalla are present as the breadth of that longing (a gathering under the aurora; the wolf lying down beside
  the doe), never as separate places.
- **What changes:** she begins as a witness of a God who *moved*, out there in the past, in the glory of creation, and
  ends as someone met by a God who *moves*, here and now, in the one place she could not show anyone. She begins
  reaching out to be welcomed and ends offering welcome.
- **What we discover with her:** the presence that hovered over the waters at the beginning hovers over the waters
  of her own eyes. Her seams remain, and she already fits.

### The emotional spine

Longing → Wonder → Kinship → Absence → Descent → Met in the dark → Belonging → Invitation.

The first drop spends wonder and kinship in full. It deliberately leaves longing unanswered (the ring with a gap), so
that the breakdown deepens the story instead of repeating a resolution: in the first half the world answers her
outstretched hand; in the second, the answer comes from below, through her tears.

### How the end answers the beginning

| The beginning | The end |
|---|---|
| A single point of light in a black where there is nothing yet. | The same point of light, composed the same way, above a horizon that is starting to burn gold: the morning star. |
| Aurelia alone in water with no shore, turning and finding no one. | Aurelia on the shore of that water, her people beside her. |
| She floats, passive, and reaches toward a star that is impossibly far. | She stands and offers her hand, with the light resting in it, to us. |
| Her first steps on land fall on the kick, alone, climbing up from the sea. | Everyone's footsteps fall on the kick, together, walking down to the sea. |
| The whispered voice of "In the beginning" over the deep (S02). | The same whisper, "God moves", as the light leaves her hand and comes to us (S39). |
| Her face over the dark water (S02). | Her face over the same water in the dark place (S27), then turned from the sea to the viewer (S38). |

### The shape, and where the land test sits

- **Loudness by section** (median short-term LUFS): the intro is the quietest (−24.3) and the spoken word next
  (−20.0). Everything from the groove-in onwards sits between −17 and −12.6. The breakdown (−15.8) is the one real
  valley after the first minute. Pre-chorus 2 (−12.9) and drop 2 (−12.6) are the peak. The last chord is the darkest
  sound in the song (a spectral centroid of 799 Hz, against 1,600–2,900 Hz in every section after the intro).
- **The approved land test** (`aurelia-land-v2`) starts its audio at 123.986758 s: on the downbeat of bar 66, the last
  bar before drop 1. In this manuscript that bar is the end of S14, the held look between Aurelia and the doe before

  (S16) but moves the contact later, after a world-waking first phrase (S15), so that it lands on the return after

### PROLOGUE · THE DEEP

*Intro and spoken word. Waves, a low drone, no drums. Bars 1–18.*

**Cadence.** No drums: the pulse is felt, not heard. Mostly two-beat holds, changing on the half-bar, and the picture
turns with the whisper's phrases rather than the waves. Never hold the same water twice; move the camera's bearing
instead.

#### S01 · The point of light
`0:00.00–0:09.16 · bars 1.0–5.7 · 19 beats · ≈12 states` · *waves and drone, no words*

Black. Not the black of a screen: a black with weight and temperature, the black under deep water at night.

A single point of light appears, far off, so small we are not sure we saw it. It stays. It is the first thing.

Below it, the dark begins to show a surface. The faintest silver catches on the back of a swell, then on another:
this is water, and it has no edge. Across that surface a ripple travels from nowhere toward nowhere. There is no
wind. Something is moving over the face of the deep, and the water feels it before we can see it.

**Coverage:** the point of light alone (two states as it steadies); the first silver on a swell; the ripple in three
positions as it crosses frame; a lower angle across the water with the point of light reflected as a thread.

#### S02 · In the beginning
`0:09.16–0:14.08 · bars 5.7–8.3 · 10 beats · ≈6 states` · *"In the beginning."* (0:09.16, whispered, close)

Close on a face, eyes closed, rising out of the dark as the silver finds it: Aurelia, at rest in the water to her
collarbones, her hair spread around her like weed. Drops on her lashes. Her lips are parted; she is breathing slowly,
the way a sleeper breathes. A drop falls from her chin, and the ring it makes travels out across the black.

**Coverage:** the approved ocean portrait is the identity anchor here (`picturebook-v1/01-reference.png`), but
closer and darker: lashes, a drop, her mouth, the ring spreading. Hold faces, change the light on them.

#### S03 · Before the light. Before the land.
`0:14.08–0:23.84 · bars 8.3–13.4 · 20 beats · ≈13 states` · *"Before the light. Before the land."* (0:14.08)

Her eyes open. She does not know where she is, and she is not afraid yet. Her hands surface, fingers spread, and rest
on the skin of the water the way you would rest them on a sleeping animal. She turns her head one way: water. The
other way: water. She turns her whole body, slowly, a full circle, and there is nothing in any direction but the deep
and the stars.

*Her thought: Where is the edge of this? Where is anyone?*

**Coverage:** eyes opening (two states); hands breaking the surface; the turn in four bearings (front, profile, rear
three-quarter, the far side) so the camera moves around her as she turns, never repeating a view; one wide from
water level that shows no horizon line at all.

#### S04 · There was only the deep
`0:23.84–0:34.48 · bars 13.4–19.0 · 22 beats · ≈14 states` · *"There was only the deep."* (0:23.84)

Wide and high: a tiny figure in a sea without a shore, under a Milky Way that fills the sky. She lets herself fall
back and floats, arms open, hair fanning, face to the stars. It looks like surrender and it is also longing. The point
of light from the first frame is directly above her. She lifts one hand toward it, and it is impossibly far away.

Under her back, the water begins to change. Something firm. A stone.

**Coverage:** the high wide (two states as she lies back); a side view at water level, her profile and the stars;
her raised hand against the sky with the point of light beyond her fingertips (the first reach, unanswered); her
foot, toes curling against a stone under the water (the hinge into Act One, on the last beat before the kick).

### ACT ONE · THE LAND APPEARS

*The kick and bass enter, then verse 1. Bars 19–42.*

**Cadence.** Four on the floor at walking pace, and the kick is her footsteps: one-beat states for steps and hands,
two-beat states for her face and for every new place. Verse 1 sings one line every four bars, each with a pickup; make
the line's new idea land on its downbeat, not on the pickup.

#### S05 · The first footstep
`0:34.48–0:49.72 · bars 19–26 · 32 beats · ≈20 states` · ◆ *kick and bass enter at 0:34.48* · *instrumental*

On the first kick, her foot finds the stone and stands. The kick is a heartbeat, and from now on it is her footsteps.

She stands. The water that held her up falls away from her body as the sea bed rises: to her waist, to her thighs.
"Let the dry land appear": the shore lifts out of the black, wet stones shining, foam drawing back off them for the
first time. Aurelia wades out, the dress heavy and dark and clinging, water pouring from her sleeves and hair. She
slips, catches herself on a rock with both hands, laughs once in surprise at her own clumsiness, and climbs. On the
stones she stands upright, streaming, and looks back at the water that held her. Then up: a path of rock and a slope
of pale grass climbing toward the stars.

Behind her, at the edge of frame, a sea-crag stands out of the water. Something rests at the top of it in a dark nest.
We will not notice it yet.

**Coverage:** see Key sequence A (section 5.1).

#### S06 · No shape, no form, no sound
`0:49.72–0:57.33 · bars 27–30 · 16 beats · ≈10 states` · *"No shape, no form, no sound"* (0:49.01)

The cliff path in near darkness. She climbs by touch: a palm flat on cold rock, fingers finding a crack, a bare foot
on lichen. Shapes around her are only half made: the land is there but not yet distinct. Everything is hushed. She
holds her breath to listen, and there is nothing to hear.

Then the first grass blade brushes the back of her hand. She stops and looks at it as if it were the first grass
anyone has ever seen.

**Coverage:** hands and feet on rock (close, tactile); her face in profile listening; the first blade against her
knuckles, extreme close; a rear view as she reaches the top of the path.

#### S07 · Darkness on the deep
`0:57.33–1:04.95 · bars 31–34 · 16 beats · ≈10 states` · *"Darkness on the deep"* (0:57.31)

At the top she turns and looks back. Below her lies the sea she came out of, black to the horizon, a skin of starlight
on it. The crag stands in the water at the foot of the cliff. Her wet hair drips on the rock. She hugs her arms against
the cold. It is beautiful and it is very large, and she was alone in it.

She turns away from it, inland, to whatever is next.

**Coverage:** over her shoulder to the sea (two bearings); her face, cold, awed; the crag from her eyeline (its nest a
dark shape); a turn, her back to the sea, her face to the land.

#### S08 · Waters with no shore
`1:04.95–1:12.57 · bars 35–38 · 16 beats · ≈10 states` · *"Waters with no shore"* (1:04.77)

The meadow. A sea of silver-gold grass runs away from her under the stars to a line of mountains, and the wind moves
it in long swells exactly like the water she left. Waters with no shore: the same deep, now made of seed heads.

She walks into it. This is our *Gladiator* moment and it must be felt: her open hand trailing through the grass at hip
height, seed heads bending and springing back between her fingers, chaff catching on her damp sleeve, her palm
tingling. The first cyan-gold lights lift off the grass where her hand has passed, like pollen made of light, and
drift up a little way and stay.

Ahead stand two landmarks we will come back to: a tall **forked standing stone**, split into two prongs from the top,
and on the rise beyond it a small **windswept tree**.

**Coverage:** a rear three-quarter wide of her entering the grass; her hand in the seed heads in three states (the
brush, the spring-back, the lights rising); a low grass-level view looking up at her profile; a wide with the forked
stone and tree on the horizon (the approved meadow geography).

#### S09 · The world was still asleep
`1:12.57–1:20.19 · bars 39–42 · 16 beats · ≈10 states` · *"The world was still asleep"* (1:12.29)

Everything here is asleep, and she is the only one awake.

At the foot of the forked stone a **doe** lies curled in the grass, legs folded, nose tucked, breathing. In a hollow
nearby a **fox kit** sleeps with its tail over its nose. Far back toward the sea, on top of the crag, the **eagle** sits
on its nest with its head under its wing.

Aurelia freezes so as not to wake them. Then, with great care and a delighted, suppressed smile, she tiptoes, a finger
to her lips as if the whole world were a nursery. She steps over the fox kit's hollow. The kit opens one eye, watches
her bare foot pass, and closes it again. She has to press her hand over her mouth so she does not laugh.

**Coverage:** the doe asleep by the stone (one wide, one close on the breathing flank); the fox kit (close, the one eye
opening on a downbeat); the eagle asleep on the crag (far, small, a silhouette against the stars); Aurelia tiptoeing
(rear, then front with finger to lips); the hand-over-mouth laugh.

### ACT TWO · SOMETHING STIRRED

*Pre-chorus 1 and build 1. The arpeggio rises; the build thins toward the drop and the riser climbs. Bars 43–66.*

**Cadence.** The pre-chorus climbs with the arpeggio: two-one-one patterns. Its two bass-out bars (49–50) are a held
breath, near-still states with one small change each. The build starts at two-beat holds and compresses as the low end
thins and the riser climbs, to single beats by bars 63–66, then one two-beat picture on the riser's peak.

#### S10 · Above the sea
`1:20.19–1:27.80 · bars 43–46 · 16 beats · ≈10 states` · *"But something stirred above the sea"* (1:19.67)

On the crag above the sea, the eagle wakes. It lifts its head, shakes out its feathers and stands over its nest,
where a single downy eaglet is pushing up toward it. The great bird opens its wings and hangs there over its young,
fluttering, not leaving, hovering in the wind off the sea.

This is the image the song's Hebrew is built on: the word "moved" in Genesis is the word for an eagle hovering over
its nest. We do not explain it. We show it, and later we will put Aurelia under the wings.

Aurelia, at the meadow's seaward edge, sees it. She stops with her hand on a seed head and watches the bird hover
over its young with an expression we will understand later: she is watching something she wants.

**Coverage:** the eagle waking (close, far side of the nest); wings opening over the eaglet (two states as they
spread); the hover against the stars (a wide that places crag, sea and sky); Aurelia's face watching, then her hand
still in the grass.

#### S11 · Something breathing
`1:27.80–1:35.42 · bars 47–50 · 16 beats · ≈10 states` · *"Something breathing, something free"* (1:27.54) · ◆ *the bass drops out at 1:31.61 (bar 49)*

A breath crosses the meadow. The grass leans toward her in one long, soft swell, and where it passes, lights lift. The
doe raises its head by the stone, ears turning. Aurelia's hair lifts off her neck. She closes her eyes and breathes
in with it, her ribs rising under the damp dress.

#### S14 · Upon the waters

### ACT THREE · GOD MOVED

*Drop 1. Full bass, vocal chop. Bars 67–82.*

#### S15 · God moved
`2:05.89–2:13.51 · bars 67–70 · 16 beats · ≈10 states` · ◆ *the drop at 2:05.89* · *"God moved"* (sung into the drop from 2:05.14) · *"(God moved)"* (2:08.97)

#### S16 · The doe
`2:13.51–2:28.74 · bars 71–78 · 32 beats · ≈20 states` · *"God moved"* (2:12.77) · *"(God moved)"* (2:16.56), then instrumental · ◆ *the one-bar dip at 2:19.22

The benchmark scene. The approved test is its seed: profile, from behind, from beside the doe, the open palm, the

On the dip, the music lifts its weight away and there is nothing but the two of them: she lifts her hand and opens it,
palm up, and holds it out, not reaching all the way. The doe stretches its neck toward her hand. Its nostrils flare.
It can smell the sea on her.

When the bass returns, it closes the distance itself. Its muzzle touches her palm.

Small cyan-gold lights rise from the point of contact and from the grass around them, as if the meadow were
exhaling. Aurelia's eyes fill. She laughs, softly, at the warmth and wet of a living nose in her hand. The doe pushes

**Coverage:** see Key sequence B (section 5.2).

### ACT FOUR · THE YAWNING GAP

*Verse 2 and pre-chorus 2. The groove stays full; the colour cools. Bars 83–98.*

**Cadence.** Verse 2 sings a line every two bars over the full groove, so the story moves at twice the pace of verse
1: one event per line, about five states each. Pre-chorus 2 mirrors pre-chorus 1, and its two bass-out bars (97–98)

#### S18 · Chaos
`2:36.36–2:40.17 · bars 83–84 · 8 beats · ≈5 states` · *"The Greeks would call it Chaos"* (2:36.09)

Still on her back, smiling, she touches another star, and another. The lines draw again, bigger this time: a ring of

**Coverage:** the ring drawing (two states); its gap, close; her fingertip at the gap (the reach from S04 again); her

#### S19 · The yawning gap

**Coverage:** her eyeline down from the ring to the horizon (a tilt told in two pictures); the rift from a distance

#### S20 · Not forgotten

The wolf comes along the rim toward her, head low. She goes very still. Her fingers close.

**Coverage:** the doe stopping at the stone, watching (rear of the doe, Aurelia walking away beyond); Aurelia at the

#### S21 · Waiting on His lap

### ACT SIX · THE SPIRIT MOVES

*Build 2. No drums at first: the long phrases rise over the pads' low swells; a kick-and-snare roll arrives with the riser
in the last four bars. The present tense arrives. Bars 108–124.*

**Cadence.** The voice sings one long phrase every four bars (108, 112, 116); let each phrase reveal one stage of the
light. Two-beat holds while she discovers it; then, over the riser (121–124), compress to single beats and end on one

#### S30 · Moves (i): the tear lands
`3:23.97–3:31.59 · bars 108–111 · 16 beats · ≈10 states` · *"And the Spirit moves"* (3:24.33)

The tear lands on the ice. Where it lands, deep under the surface, a point of cyan-gold light wakes. It is the same
light that answered her hand in the grass and the doe's muzzle in her palm, but it has never come from *below* before.
It moves. It hovers under the ice beneath her bowed head the way the eagle hovered over its nest.

She does not see it yet. Then the light shows through her fingers, and she lowers her hands.

**Coverage:** the tear meeting the ice (close); the point of light waking below (two states, brighter); her hands over

#### S31 · Moves (ii): the seams fill
`3:31.59–3:39.20 · bars 112–115 · 16 beats · ≈10 states` · *"And the Spirit moves"* (3:32.02)

The light runs out along one crack in the ice, and then another, and then every crack at once, until the whole
mosaic is outlined in living gold. Her reflection is still in pieces. The cracks have not closed. But every seam
between the pieces is lit, and the broken face in the ice is a face made of light.

She stares at it. She touches her real cheek again, wet, and the light in the seams brightens under her touch.

**Coverage:** the light along one crack, then many (three states from above, so the mosaic visibly fills); her

#### S32 · Upon my waters
`3:39.20–3:46.82 · bars 116–119 · 16 beats · ≈10 states` · *"And the Spirit moves upon my waters"* (3:39.65)

surface of her own deep. And she weeps, openly now, and her tears are full of light. They gleam on her cheeks like the
seams in the ice; when they fall, they fall as lights. These are *my waters*: the deep the Spirit moves on is the pool,
and it is her own eyes.

The lit seams run outward from the pool into the rock and up the walls of the rift, veining the dark with gold in every
direction. High above, on the ledge, two eyes catch the light: the wolf never left. Higher still, across the slit of

*Her thought: It was here. Down here too.*

**Coverage:** her palm on the ice, the light gathering under it (two states); her tears shining, one falling as a light;

### ACT SEVEN · GOD MOVES

*Drop 2. Full bass, euphoric; the key lifts. Bars 125–132.*

**Cadence.** The loudest and shortest section: eight bars, the densest picture rate in the film, a mix of one- and
two-beat states. Every call lands a new event: the rising, the doe, the people, the ring.

#### S34 · God moves
`3:56.34–4:03.96 · bars 125–128 · 16 beats · ≈10 states` · ◆ *the drop at 3:56.34* · *"God moves"* (sung into the drop from 3:55.57) · *"(God moves)"* (3:59.40)

The light goes up out of the gap like breath out of lungs. The rift blazes from end to end, gold along every seam, and
Aurelia comes up out of it: up the path, past the wolf (it rises and comes with her), onto the rim, into the open.
The meadow is lit as it was in the first drop, but there is something new in the sky: low on the horizon toward the
sea, under the stars, the first rose and gold of a dawn.

She is not made new in the sense of polished. Her hem is torn and muddy from the climb, her palm is scraped, her face
is wet and shining. She has never looked more beautiful.

And the doe is already running. Across the meadow, through the grass, straight to her, and this time no one offers a
hand: the doe pushes its forehead into her chest and she wraps her arms around its neck.

**Coverage:** see Key sequence E (section 5.5).

#### S35 · The circle
`4:03.96–4:11.58 · bars 129–132 · 16 beats · ≈10 states` · *"God moves"* (4:03.20) · *"(God moves)"* (4:07.03)

People are coming across the meadow. From every direction, out of the dawn light and the grass, figures walk
toward her, and they are the perfected people of God's kingdom: human, every one of them, and whole. No age on them is
frailty and no mark on them is pain. An elder, silver-haired and upright, laughing; a man with a small child on his
shoulders; two friends arm in arm; a woman who breaks into a run. Their skin is warm and real, and light lives in it
and in their simple pale clothes: the same cyan and gold that answered Aurelia's touch all night, now shining out of
them from within. They are angelic in their light, not in wings. They are everyone she loves, as the kingdom makes
them. She does not have to be told.

The film's quiet paradox must be seen here: they are perfect, and she is not. Her hem is torn, her palm is scraped and
her face is wet, and nothing in their faces asks her to be otherwise. The light that shines from them is the light that
filled her cracks in the dark. What makes them radiant is already in her seams.

The first to arrive takes her face in both hands. Then everyone. Embraces, foreheads pressed together, laughter
through tears.

They draw her toward the forked stone, where the others have already joined hands around it in a ring. There is
one place in the ring with no one in it, between the elder and the child, and both of them are holding out a
hand toward her. She steps into it. Their hands close on hers.

Above them, in the ring of figures in the sky, the missing star ignites, and the circle is whole. The eagle comes down
onto the windswept tree on the rise and opens its wings over all of them.

**Coverage:** see Key sequence E (section 5.5).

`4:26.81–4:40.14 · bars 141–147 · 28 beats · ≈18 states` · ◆ *the last chord at 4:26.81, "God moves" sung onto it*

She turns from the sea to us. The doe is at her side, her people behind her on the shore, radiant, the dawn behind them

She looks into the lens the way she looked at the doe across the grass: openly, unhurried, wanting nothing from us

#### S39 · The morning star

The light lifts from her palm. On the whisper, the same voice that began the film saying "In the beginning", it

## 5. Key sequences in picture states

Six sequences, written state by state so Codex can see how an action develops in one- and two-beat pictures without
becoming a string of unrelated illustrations. The start times come from the beat grid. Each sequence's states fill its
scenes' beats exactly (the script that renders these tables checks it). Everywhere else, the manuscript's coverage
notes give the progression for Codex to expand at the same density.

The rules these follow:
- **One idea per state, and something changes every time**: a bearing, a distance, a gesture, the light. Two states
  in a row from the same bearing must differ in action.
- **Contacts, reveals and releases land on the ◆ anchors**, or on the downbeat of a bar when there is none.
- **Two-beat states for faces and for anything we must read; one-beat states for detail and momentum.**
- **The approved land frames are the seed of the doe sequence**, not its whole: the encounter grows new beats
  (the hesitation, the kneel, the dip-bar offer, the push for more, the stroke, the standing together).

## 6. Continuity and motif ledger

### Aurelia

**Identity (fixed):** an adult woman with olive skin and long, dark, wavy-to-curly hair. Her opaque charcoal-blue
dress is long-sleeved, long and flowing, and softly belted. She is barefoot throughout (proposed: it makes every
contact with grass, rock, ice and foam tactile). The identity references are the ocean portrait
(`picturebook-v1/01-reference.png`) and the meadow set (`aurelia-land-v1/03`, `05`, `06`, `07`, `08`).

### The perfected people (Gareth: "angelic forms of the personified perfect of God's kingdom. Still human, just perfect.")

- **Who:** everyone Aurelia loves, as the kingdom makes them. Original and unnamed (no real people, none of Gareth's
  relatives). About a dozen. Three have readable faces and recur: **the elder** (silver-haired, upright, laughing; she
  stands to Aurelia's right in the ring), **the child** (to her left in the ring; the fox kit's playmate) and **the
  friend** (the first to arrive, who takes Aurelia's face in her hands). The rest are seen at a distance and in groups.
- **How perfection looks:** wholeness and light, not smoothness. Real skin, real age on the elder, real laughter; no
  frailty, no wounds, no wings. Light lives in their skin and in their plain, pale, flowing clothes (ivory, warm
  white, soft gold, each person's distinct), and the cyan-gold lights move around them. Angelic in their light, human
  in their bodies.
- **Against Aurelia:** she keeps her charcoal-blue dress, torn hem, scraped palm and wet face. She does not change
  clothes and does not become radiant like them. The only rhyme between her and them is the gold in her tear tracks:
  the same light. That contrast is the film's statement of belonging, so it must never be resolved by "fixing" her.
- **Appearances:** they arrive and form the ring (S35); walk down to the sea (S36); stand on the shore (S37); stand
  behind her, looking at us, as she offers her palm (S38).

### Motifs and their payoffs

- **The point of light.** First frame (S01): unreachable, alone. S04: she reaches for it. S38: one rests in her palm,
  offered to us. S39: on the whispered "God moves" it comes to us, and the last picture is the morning star,
  composed as the first frame was.
- **Water.** The shoreless sea (S01–S04) becomes a sea of grass (S08: "waters with no shore"), then the small black
  pool in the gap (S27: "the same deep"), then her tears (S32: "my waters"), and at last a sea with a shore (S37). The
  ocean is brief and bookends the film; on land, its meaning travels in other forms.
- **The ring with a gap.** Drawn in the stars in S18 with one figure missing; framed overhead by the walls of the rift
  in S33, its gap directly above her; completed in S35 when she steps into the ring of hands around the forked stone
  and the missing star ignites.
- **The mosaic.** The land equivalent of the earlier fragmented-water idea is the crazed ice over the pool (S28): her
  reflection in pieces. In S31 the seams fill with gold and do not close. Its echoes are the forked stone (broken and
  standing) and the faint gold in her tear tracks at the end. The circle of distinct people, each one different, is
  the mosaic made of persons.
- **Hovering.** The ripple with no source on the water (S01), the eagle over its eaglet (S10), the wind on the face
  of the sea (S12), the eagle over Aurelia (S23), the light hovering under the ice (S30), the eagle over the gathering
  (S35).
- **The kick as footsteps.** Her first steps alone (S05) and everyone's steps together (S36).
- **The whisper.** The close, spoken voice of "In the beginning" (S02) returns once, as the film's last words: the
  whispered "God moves" (S39). The film gives that whisper to the moment the light reaches the viewer.
- **Answering.** Every touch in the first half gets an answer from the world; the gap (S18) is the first that does
  not; the answer that finally comes (S30–S32) comes from beneath.

### Resonances: subtext for the makers, never captions

- **Genesis 1:1–2**, the Spirit "moved" upon the face of the waters; the Hebrew *merachefet* is the hovering of
  **Deuteronomy 32:11**'s eagle over its young (S10, S23, S35).
- **Genesis 1:9**, "let the dry land appear" (S05).
- **Hesiod's Chaos** and the Norse **Ginnungagap**, the yawning gap between ice and fire (S19–S33). The wolf has
  Norse company too, and an eagle sits in the crown of the Norse world-tree (the windswept tree, S35). These lend
  weight; none is spelled out.
- **Psalm 139:11–12**, "the darkness hideth not from thee": the wolf's eyes on the ledge were there all along (S32).
- **2 Corinthians 4:6**, the light commanded out of darkness, "shined in our hearts": the light rising from beneath
  the ice (S30–S32).
- **Job 38:7**, "when the morning stars sang together": the last picture (S39).

## 7. Established direction, creative proposals and production questions

### 7.1 What this manuscript takes as established (Gareth and the handover)

- Aurelia's identity and costume; her sensuousness as living in her senses; beauty that includes vulnerability,
  effort, tears and laughter.
- The ocean only briefly at the beginning; the main action on land against an immense star field; colour beyond
  nature, with texture and darkness kept.
- Effects are relationships: every light answers a touch, and nature may start the exchange.
- Animals with agency. The deer contact is the emotional benchmark.
- The touched star revealing a constellation, on land.
- Mosaic as an emotional motif with a land equivalent, not a recipe.
- The forked standing stone and the windswept tree as recurring landmarks.
- No invented bereavement, diagnosis or biography for Aurelia; unnamed loved ones only; none of Gareth's relatives.
- No added voice, dialogue or music. Interior lines here are direction only.
- Held, reference-led pictures; no unchanged picture longer than two beats; contacts, reveals and releases on musical
  accents.
- The moved→moves revelation is protected: the breakdown deepens and transforms the story.
- A 4K60 delivery with the original song.

**Gareth's decisions on the first draft (13 October 2026):**

- **No lyrics on screen.** "This one carries its meaning in the visual/audio only."
- **Keep the dawn** in drop 2 and the epilogue, with the stars still out.
- **The loved ones are "angelic forms of the personified perfect of God's kingdom. Still human, just perfect."**
  Written into S35–S38 and the ledger (section 6) as perfected, radiant people without wings.
- **Keep the wolf.** ("Wolves are cool.")

### 7.2 What this manuscript proposes (Claude's, for Gareth to accept or refuse)

Each proposal has a fallback that keeps the scene's feeling if it is refused.

1. **The ring of figures with a gap** (S18, S33, S35) as the shape of her longing and its answer. *Fallback:* a single
   constellation of two hands almost touching, completed at the end.
2. **The gap in the earth** (S19–S33): Chaos and Ginnungagap staged as a real rift between an ice wall and an ember-lit
   rim, entered by a path, with a pool at the bottom. *Fallback:* the breakdown happens in the cleft of the forked stone
   at night, with a frost-skinned puddle at its foot; the same coverage applies, at smaller scale.
3. **The cracked-ice mosaic and the gold seams** (S28–S31) as the mosaic motif on land: her reflection stays in pieces,
   and the seams fill with light rather than closing. *Fallback:* a dry, crazed mud-flat or a cracked slab of slate
   at the bottom of the gap, the light rising through its cracks; the reflection is then lost, so keep the matched
   S02/S27 close with her face lit from below instead.
4. **The eagle and the Hebrew hovering** (S10, S23, S35). *Fallback:* drop the eagle and carry the hovering in wind and
   light alone (grass swells, the hair lift, the light hovering under the ice).
5. **The epilogue on the shore**, everyone walking down to the sea on the outro's kick, the invitation with the sea
   behind her. *Fallback:* the invitation in the meadow at the forked stone, the circle behind her.
6. **The final invitation to the lens**, with the scraped palm and one light resting in it (S38), and the light
   coming to us on the whispered "God moves" (S39). This is the recommendation most worth keeping: it makes the last
   "God moves" happen to the person watching, and it is the deer encounter turned toward the audience.
7. **A matched first and last picture**: one point of light above water, first in nothing, last over a gold horizon.
8. **Bare feet, a torn hem and a scraped palm** as the visible, unmended marks of her journey.
9. **Footsteps on the kick** (S05 and S36), and the drop's one-bar dip (S16) and the build's bass-out bar (S33) as the
   held breaths before contact and before "God moves".

### 7.3 Difficult shots and alternates

| Shot | The risk | The alternate that keeps the feeling |
|---|---|---|
| Fingertip touching a star (S17) | Forced perspective can read as a finger near a dot. | Shoot it from behind her raised hand with the star just above the fingertip and let the *next* state show the star brighter and the first line drawn; or show the light in her fingertip and the star brightening in a separate cut. |
| The constellations drawing (S17, S18, S35) | Line-art over a photographic sky can look like a graphic. | Draw the figures with light itself: brighter stars and faint luminous haze along the lines, closer to an aurora filament than to a diagram. Reveal over two or three states. |
| The doe running to her (S34) | Motion in a still can freeze awkwardly. | Show the doe's run in a low side view with grass in the air, then cut straight to the forehead-to-chest contact; skip the deceleration. |
| The wolf's head in her lap (S21) | Scale and anatomy of a large animal in a lap. | Frame from above on her hands and the top of its head; or have it lie alongside her, its chin on her knee. |
| The eagle hovering low over her (S23) | Wings over a person can look like an attack. | Keep the bird higher, its spread wings filling the sky above her face, and sell the closeness with the downdraught in her hair and dress. |
| Light filling cracks in ice (S30–S31) | The crack pattern must stay the same across states. | Generate the cracked-ice plate once and grade the light into it per state, so only the light changes; this is also the most economical coverage in the film. |
| Climbing down and out of the gap (S25, S34) | Dark, busy rock can be illegible. | Use hands and feet in close-up for the effort, and wide silhouettes against the slit of stars or the lit seams for the geography. |
| The perfected people (S35–S38) | "Perfect" people can come out airbrushed, plastic or like a stock crowd; many faces must stay consistent; Aurelia must remain the face that acts. | Show perfection as wholeness and light, not smoothness: real skin, real age on the elder, light from within. Backlight them against the dawn; give only three readable faces (the elder, the child, the friend) and keep each one's pale clothes plain and distinct. |
| The scraped palm offered to the lens (S38) | Hands have been the hardest part of every method so far. | Use the approved open-palm pose from the deer touch, turned toward camera; if the frontal palm fails, show the palm in profile with the light on it, then her eyes in the lens. |
| Dawn and full stars together (S34–S39) | Can wash out the star field. | Keep the glow below the lowest tenth of the sky; the stars stay dense above. |

### 7.4 Questions for Gareth

1. **No wings?** I have read "angelic forms… still human" as radiant people without wings: angelic in their light,
   human in their bodies. Say if you want wings, or a suggestion of them in the light behind them.
2. **Dissolves in the coda.** The last 22 seconds (S38–S39) are one sustained chord with waves and a whisper. The
   manuscript keeps the two-beat ceiling there and advances small changes. Would you like to test slow cross-dissolves
   between those states instead? That is untested, so it is a question rather than a direction.
3. **Faster holds in the builds.** Bars 65–66 and 121–124 could use half-beat holds if a test shows they read. Test
   them?
4. **The title card.** None is written. With no lyrics on screen, a title may be the only text in the film; if you
   want one, its natural place is the bass entry at 0:34.48, over the first footstep, or after the morning star.

### 7.5 Picture count and economy

At an average hold of 1.6 beats, the whole film needs about **380 picture states** (607 beats; section 4 adds them up
scene by scene). Many states are cheap
variations of a plate, which makes them economical to generate:

- **Graded states of one plate:** the light filling the cracks (S30–S32), the dawn brightening over the sea (S39),
  the held breath of S11.
- **Re-posed states in one place:** the doe encounter, the wolf, the invitation.
- **Distinct plates:** the shore, the cliff path, the meadow wides, the gap's rim, the gap's floor, the shore at
  dawn. About two dozen in all.

**What is solid and what is not.**

- **Solid:** the tempo, the grid and the bar numbers; the section boundaries (each is a step change in the bass or
  drums on a bar line); the hard anchors (◆).
- **Good to about a tenth of a second:** the sung lines' starts, measured from the vocal stem and cross-checked with
  whisper. Suno's own embedded lyric timings were used only for the text and as a first draft.
- **By ear, not by measurement:** the key change. The source asked for A minor to C major; the whole take reads A
  minor, and per-section key estimates are too noisy on this mix to confirm the lift into drop 2. Gareth's ear decides.
- **Not established:** frame-exact word sync. The handover notes that Codex's automated alignment failed on the
  repeated lines and is quarantined; this manuscript needs only line cues, and gives them as cues.
