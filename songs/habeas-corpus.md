# Habeas Corpus

**Slot:** to be decided. **Style:** posh British battle rap over colossal FM dubstep sub bass, about 140 bpm, four
sixteen-bar rounds with sixteen-bar instrumentals between them, no chorus. First try, 16 October 2026.

The writ of *habeas corpus* ("you shall have the body") orders whoever is holding a prisoner to produce him in court.
The Grave is served the writ. It cannot comply.

## Where the shiver lands

Battle rap has one engine: the MC boasts, and the opponent is demolished. Worship forbids the first half ("let him
who boasts boast in the Lord"), and that turns out to be the song's trick: every boast is on behalf of the King, the
narrator admits every charge against himself in round three, and the only opponent Scripture lets you taunt is the
one on trial. Paul taunts death in 1 Corinthians 15; Elijah taunts Baal on Carmel. So the demolition is real and the
target is the Accuser and the Grave, and the last line turns the demolition into an invitation: the stone that sealed
the tomb is propping the door open.

The four rounds are an argument in order: the herald's credentials (round 1), the King's (round 2), the trial, where
the cross is a legal reversal (round 3), and the empty tomb, where the writ is served (round 4). The final exchange
answers the title and the taunt: no body was produced, and nobody has found the sting.

## The cast

- **The Knight Commander**: the herald. He boasts only in his Liege and calls himself guilty.
- **Bartholomew**: his squire, in the Jenkins role. Named for the apostle; dry as a biscuit.
- **The Accuser**: the defendant. "Satan" is Hebrew for "the accuser", so his name is a job description.
- **The Grave**: his only witness, and it cannot produce the body.

## Production notes

- **Keep every `[Instrumental: Sixteen Bars]` block.** The breathing space between rounds is the structure, as in the
  reference. If Suno shortens them, the tags are the first thing to reinforce. The tag lines are compressed to keep the
  lyric block under Suno's limit; the reference's fuller tags (rubber FM wobbles, cavernous tape echoes, huge silent
  gaps, slow filter sweeps, no melodic lead) say the same things and the style prompt carries them.
- **Suno may try to add a chorus.** The intro says "No chorus" and the exclude list says it again; if a take still
  grows one, regenerate rather than keep it.
- **Pronunciations Suno may fumble:** *tetelestai* (teh-TEL-es-tie), *Pleiades* (PLY-a-deez), *ex nihilo* (eks
  NEE-hi-lo), *periwig*, *scarpered*. If *tetelestai* breaks a take, the line can read: `Stamped "It is finished" on
  it: in the Greek, a receipt for "paid in full",`.
- **The fast verses.** Lines run 14–20 syllables; at triple-time the reference's MC stays intelligible, and clarity
  depends on the take. If words blur, ask for 135 bpm or mark verse 2 double-time and keep triple-time for verse 4.
- **Length:** the lyric block is under Suno's 5,000-character limit and the style prompt under 200 (counted on
  16 October 2026). The reference lyric is 4,999 characters: its author trimmed it to the limit, so every line here
  was tightened to fit, which also helps the words land at speed.
- **Register:** British throughout (CV, short-let, periwig, scarpered, peckish, "with costs"). No profanity: the
  demolition is carried by the argument, and its target is the Accuser and the Grave, never a person.
