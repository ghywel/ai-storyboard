# Sponsor Me

**Working title:** *Sponsor Me: a World Vision plea for help*. **Style:** slow British live worship rock in the spirit
of a stripped live set: sing-spoken verses over a metronome kick and one chiming guitar, a chorus that soars, a
bridge that builds to a congregation. First draft, 19 October 2026.

**The brief (Gareth, 19 October 2026):** an emotive plea for World Vision's child sponsorship, made as a gift to the
charity for its social media. Emotionally devastating: it starts from the truth of what these children endure and
connects the viewers with their own guilt for standing by. It builds to a glorious, fruitful future where no child
is left behind and poverty, skin colour and country of origin mean nothing. It moves from desolation to the
welcome of God's kingdom: a Christian message that challenges people who are not religious without alienating them.
Inspiration: a haunting live worship song with sing-spoken verses, a soaring chorus, a simple drum metronome and a
guitar that builds. Full design: `films/sponsor-me/sponsor-me-design.md`.

## The shape

The song is one parent's confession that turns into a vow, and the vow becomes everyone's.

1. **Her morning** (verse 1). Five a.m. before the cockerel, a seven-year-old on the road with a yellow water can.
   Every line is a picture, and none of them is her body: the can, the dust, the brown water, a cough at night, the
   school she walks past.
2. **Our morning** (verse 2). Seven a.m. in a green and pleasant land: the hot tap, the cereal, the slow Wi-Fi, two
   pairs of school shoes by the door. Then her face on the phone, and the thumb that scrolls on by.
3. **The cockerel** (pre-chorus). Once, twice, three times he scrolls past, and a cockerel cries. A Christian hears
   Peter's three denials before the cock crowed (Matthew 26:34, 75). Anyone else hears a man admitting the same thing
   three times. The guilt is the viewer's own, taken from our side of the world, not squeezed out of her suffering.
4. **The chorus** turns confession into worship and then into a vow. "Every face that I scrolled past was You,
   looking at me" is Matthew 25:40 and 45 ("whatever you did not do for one of the least of these, you did not do for
   me") in one line. Then the title is asked in a child's voice, "Will you sponsor me?", and answered with the
   godparent's vow from the baptism service: "With the help of God, I will." *Sponsor* means one who makes a solemn
   promise (Latin *spondere*, to pledge), and in the Church a sponsor is a godparent. That makes the advert's ask and
   the Christian heart of it the same sentence.
5. **The exchange** (verse 3 and her letter). The honest arithmetic, £30 a month is a pound a day. Where it really
   goes: into the ground, a well, a nurse, a classroom roof, and four more children beside her. Then the treasure,
   child to child: a felt-tip lion, stickers and crayons in a padded envelope, and twelve weeks later her drawing
   back. Her letter is the song's only other solo voice: "I drew you my tree."
6. **The bridge** is the challenge to people who are not religious, made without a sermon: "You don't need my God to
   see a child who's been waiting forty days." Then it shows what the Christian hope looks like: the feast on the
   mountain (Isaiah 25:6), every nation, tribe and tongue (Revelation 7:9), the last made first, a chair beside her
   with your name on. "Let the little children come" (Matthew 19:14). "Whoever welcomes one of these welcomes me"
   (Mark 9:37).
7. **The final chorus** moves from *I* to *we*: the congregation takes the vow.
8. **The outro** returns to five a.m. and the cockerel, now just a new day. She is on the road with a reading book.
   The yellow can is full beside the door. She is running. A whisper: "I will."

## How the brief was handled

| In the brief | What the song does, and why |
|---|---|
| Emotionally devastating, from the truth of what children endure | The truth is told through things and distances (the can, the hour each way, the brown water, the cough, the school she can't go into), never through a child's suffering body. Charity codes on images and messages (Dóchas in Ireland, Bond in the UK) ask for dignity, and World Vision is more likely to share a film that keeps it. The devastation comes from the cut between her morning and ours |
| Connect the viewer with their own guilt for doing nothing | The singer confesses it for the viewer, in the place the viewer will be watching: scrolling past her face on a phone. The film plays in a social feed, so the confession describes what the viewer was doing a second earlier |
| Build to a glorious, fruitful future where no child is left behind | The bridge's table and the final chorus's *we*. "Fruitful" is in the picture: her drawn tree grows into the tree over the table |
| Poverty, skin colour and country of origin become meaningless | "No colour, no border, no first, no last", and in the picture one warm light on every face at the table |
| Christian, challenging the non-religious without alienating them | The verses and the arithmetic are for everyone. The chorus is plainly a prayer. The bridge speaks straight to people who don't pray, grants them the moral point ("you don't need my God to see"), then shows them the Christian hope rather than arguing for it |
| Treasure of heart and mind: puzzles, trinkets, the boys' art, books | World Vision UK asks that anything posted fits an A4 padded envelope, and suggests crayons, stickers, small toys and stationery. The song sends exactly that, so the advert promises nothing a new sponsor can't do. Books and puzzles become the felt-tip lion and the crayons |
| The visit, and one day a home in the UK | Kept out of the song. Visits come after a year's sponsorship, are arranged by World Vision with three months' notice, and other offices say they are not held in the child's home, for her protection. A UK home is Gareth's private hope, not something the programme offers, so an advert should not show it |
| The film is not about Gareth and his wife | The singer is any parent. The film's girl is invented, never the child Gareth sponsors (`films/sponsor-me/sponsor-me-design.md`, tone rules) |

## Production notes

- **Not the inspiration's words.** The lyric is new. It takes the inspiration's form (half-spoken verses, a chorus
  that soars on one long word, a slow build to a live congregation) and none of its lines. Suno refuses artist names
  in the style prompt, so the style says what the sound is, not whose it is.
- **Tempo.** The style says "slow". Measure the inspiration's tempo once its MP3 is here (`tools/song-analyze.py`)
  and add it to the style prompt as a number if the takes come out too fast or too busy.
- **The girl's voice.** Suno's child voices can sound uncanny. If her letter or the "(Will you sponsor me?)" echoes
  come out wrong, change the tag to "soft young woman's voice". If that fails too, generate her lines separately
  and lay them in the soundtrack (`tools/soundtrack.py`). The parentheses ask Suno to sing the echoes as a backing
  voice, not as the lead.
- **The congregation.** "Live worship" and "congregation" in the style prompt should bring the crowd in on the final
  chorus. If a take puts the crowd on every chorus, keep it only if the first chorus still sounds like one man.
- **Keep the hush.** The verses must stay sing-spoken and close to the mic. A take that sings them full voice spends
  the chorus too early: send it back.
- **Length.** Under Suno's 5,000-character lyric limit and 200-character style limit (3,068 and 162, counted on
  19 October 2026). Expect about four and a half to five and a half minutes. The social cutdowns in the design
  document are cut from the full take, not generated separately.
