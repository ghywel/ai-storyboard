# Same Bus, Different Stop

**Slot:** to be decided. **Style:** British rap battle over colossal FM dubstep sub bass, about 140 bpm: a male
British atheist MC against a female British Christian MC, four rounds of eight bars each way, a posh adjudicator
between rounds, no chorus. First try, 16 October 2026.

**The brief (Gareth):** brutal put-downs that dig into the real and uncomfortable flaws on both sides; the Christian
gains the upper hand in every round; at the end they agree to disagree and walk away at an impasse.

## Where the shiver lands

A battle where the Christian wins every round and converts nobody. That is the honest shape of these arguments, and
the song says so out loud: "Four rounds to Grace. Minds changed: none." The put-downs are real on both sides. Occam
lands the problem of evil, Joshua, Psalm 137, the Inquisition, Galileo, the burden of proof and the unfalsifiable
plan; Grace lands the record (what Nicaea did and didn't do, what Q is, who Paul met), the borrowed ruler, the priests
behind the Big Bang and genetics, and Nietzsche's own unanswered question. She never pretends the hard pages aren't
there: "I don't skip those pages. I read them and I grieve." And when he says she can't prove it, she agrees.

The exit is the title: same bus, different stop. Nobody has won the other over, and they leave together.

## How the brief was handled

Gareth's brief lists the atheist's case as commonly made. Several of those points are not accurate as stated, so the
song gives them to Occam in their popular form and lets Grace win the round by correcting them. That serves the brief
better than a strawman would: her upper hand is earned on the record.

| In the brief | On the record | In the song |
|---|---|---|
| The canon was assembled arbitrarily by the Council of Nicaea | Nicaea (325) ruled on the Arian question and the date of Easter; it took no action on the canon. The 27-book New Testament list appears in Athanasius's festal letter of 367 and was affirmed at Hippo (393) and Carthage (397), reflecting what the churches already used | Occam says it; Grace answers with "Dan Brown novel" and the letter of 367 |
| Four gospels from the same Q source | Q is a hypothesised sayings source for the material Matthew and Luke share and Mark lacks. Mark is earliest; John is independent | Occam says it; Grace: "Q's a guess at what two gospels share; Mark came first; John's separate" |
| The rest of the New Testament is by Paul, who never met Jesus | Thirteen letters carry Paul's name (seven undisputed); Hebrews, James, 1–2 Peter, 1–3 John, Jude, Revelation and Acts are not his. He did not meet Jesus in the flesh; he met Peter and James within a few years (Galatians 1:18–19), and the creed he passes on in 1 Corinthians 15:3–7 is dated by most scholars to within about five years of the crucifixion | Occam: "a bloke who never met Him wrote the rest"; Grace: Peter, His brother, "five years after Friday" |
| Written well after Jesus | Paul's letters from the 50s, Mark about 65–75, the rest by about 100: decades, within living memory, and early by ancient standards. The authorship of the gospels is anonymous | Occam's "forty years after the funeral by authors with no headstone" is allowed to stand; Grace answers with the creed's date, not the gospels' |
| The gods of the Old and New Testaments are irreconcilably different | This is Marcion's position (c. 144), rejected by the early church. "Love your neighbour" is Leviticus 19:18; Jonah, Hosea and Psalm 103 are mercy; Jesus speaks of judgment and Gehenna more than any other New Testament voice. The conquest passages and Psalm 137:9 are genuinely hard, and the song does not pretend otherwise | Occam: "two gods in one binding"; Grace: Marcion, Leviticus, Nineveh, and "I read them and I grieve" |
| Christianity is the only religion offering peace through forgiveness | Other traditions teach forgiveness and compassion. The distinctively Christian claim is that the wronged party pays the debt Himself | Grace's challenge is the defensible form: "name another god who pays the bill Himself" |
| Human advancement came from Christian nations, often with poor behaviour | Contested as a whole (paper, printing and the compass are Chinese; algebra and the zero came through the Islamic world and India), but the specific cases are solid: Lemaître (Big Bang), Mendel (genetics), the universities and hospitals, Wilberforce and abolition, Newton, Faraday, Kepler, Pascal. Galileo's condemnation (1633) and the Church's apology (1992) are also on the record | Grace claims the specific cases; Occam gets Galileo and the 359-year apology |
| The atheist has no proof at all | The atheist's actual position is that the burden lies on the claimant (Russell's teapot, 1952). The song gives Occam this, his strongest ground, and lets Grace answer it with the limits of method ("mute on the why and the whom") rather than by denying it | Round 3 |
| Pascal's wager, dismissed | The many-gods objection is the standard reply, and Occam makes it. Pascal's own point (Pensées 233) was that one cannot abstain: "you must wager; it is not optional" | Round 4 |
| Nietzsche contributed only the question of the moral void | The madman passage (The Gay Science §125, 1882): "God is dead… we have killed him… how shall we comfort ourselves?" Nietzsche also held that secular humanism is Christian morality without its author, which is Grace's "good on credit" line (the thesis of Tom Holland's *Dominion*). The horse in Turin (January 1889) is a well-known anecdote of uncertain reliability | Round 4 |

**Other claims in the song.** "Forty thousand denominations" is the figure commonly cited (from the World Christian
Encyclopedia's counting method, which inflates it); it is Occam's talking point. "So are the Swedes, so are the Danes"
refers to Phil Zuckerman's studies of Scandinavian secular societies. "Three hundred and fifty years to say sorry":
1633 to 1992 is 359. "A hundred and forty years" since 1882 is 144 in 2026. The "files your bishops hid" refers to the
abuse scandals; it is kept to one line and no detail, which is the only way the brand can carry it.

## Production notes

- **Two voices.** The verse tags name the voice each time (male British MC / female British MC). Suno follows these
  reasonably well in a duet; if a take swaps or blends them, regenerate rather than keep it. The adjudicator should
  stay the aristocratic spoken voice of *Habeas Corpus*, and Bartholomew is the same squire.
- **No chorus, and no instrumental breaks.** In a battle the adjudicator's verdict is the break; the first draft had
  eight-bar instrumentals between rounds and they cost 300 characters of the 5,000. The intro says "no chorus", the
  exclude list says it again; a take that grows one goes back.
- **The outro** is spoken over a heartbeat kick and rain, with the beat gone: the one moment where the two voices
  speak to each other instead of at the crowd.
- **Pronunciations Suno may fumble:** *Nicaea* (ny-SEE-a), *Athanasius*, *Lemaître* (le-METR), *Nineveh*, *Marcion*,
  *leukaemia*. Numbers are spelled out as words so they are rapped, not read.
- **No profanity.** The brutality is in the content, as asked; nothing in it is a slur, and the abuse scandal is one
  line without detail.
- **Length:** the lyric block is under Suno's 5,000-character limit and the style prompt under 200 (counted on
  16 October 2026). The first draft was 6,364: two voices and an adjudicator in the same box as one MC. Every line
  was cut to a punchline's length, which is what the form wants anyway.
